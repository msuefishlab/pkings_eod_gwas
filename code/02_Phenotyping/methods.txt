Quantitative classification of the EOD P0 phase (TP / Wobble / BP)
=================================================================

EOD waveforms were phenotyped for the presence and magnitude of the small
head-negative first phase (P0) that distinguishes triphasic (TP), "wobble", and
biphasic (BP) discharges. All analysis was restricted to Paramormyrops
kingsleyae. Each fish's replicate sweeps were peak-to-peak normalized and locally
baselined (median of a flat pre-P1 window) in MATLAB, and per-sweep measurements
plus an iZC1-anchored P0 window (the ~1 ms preceding the zero-crossing before the
P1 upswing) were exported (export_per_sweep_measurements.m). Because the absolute
size of the P0 baseline varies several-fold among populations for reasons
unrelated to phenotype, the classifier was designed so that neither axis depends
on absolute P0 size in a population-specific way, and no threshold was fit to the
by-eye phenotype labels (which were reserved for held-out validation).

P0 phenotype was resolved on two orthogonal, label-free axes. (1) Presence (BP vs
P0-present) was scored with two amplitude-invariant statistics computed on the
per-sweep-average P0 window: a shape score (maximum Pearson correlation against a
bank of small negative-Gaussian dip templates spanning the realistic range of P0
positions and widths) and dipSNR (dip depth expressed in units of the fish's own
baseline-noise MAD). A three-component Gaussian mixture model (mclust) was fit to
(shape, log dipSNR); the lowest-dipSNR component was taken as the no-P0 (BP) mode,
and presence probability computed as one minus its posterior. (2) Magnitude
(Wobble vs TP, among P0-present fish) was scored with the peak-to-peak-normalized
P0 amplitude |vP0| — the depth of the P0 relative to the whole discharge — which,
unlike dipSNR, is population-robust (~2.7-fold vs ~7-fold spread across TP
populations); the TP/Wobble boundary was placed at the data-driven antimode of a
two-component mixture on log|vP0|. Fish near either boundary were labeled Ambiguous
rather than force-classified, reflecting the continuous nature of the BP-to-TP P0
gradient. Cross-sweep P0-latency consistency was recorded as an auxiliary flag but
was not used in the call, because it is confounded by the reproducible waveform
onset (clean BP discharges are as latency-consistent as weak wobbles).

The classifier was validated against seven fish with independently confirmed
electrocyte anatomy (four TP, two wobble, one BP), which it classified correctly in
six of seven cases; the single miss was a wobble whose P0 amplitude lay at the
recording noise floor and which was flagged as unresolvable rather than misassigned.
Notably, the quantitative call corrected one anatomically-confirmed wobble that had
been mislabeled BP by eye. At the weakest-wobble margin the P0 is at or below the
resolution of the electric signal and cannot be assigned from the EOD alone; such
fish are flagged for anatomical follow-up.
